Market-Neutral Pairs Trading with Machine Learning (Capstone Thesis)

Tests whether XGBoost, LightGBM and LSTM signals can beat a classic cointegration pairs-trading strategy, on 105 US large caps over 2010-2025.

Python XGBoost LightGBM LSTM Statistical Arbitrage
  • Capstone thesis for the MSc in Financial Engineering at WorldQuant University (supervised by Saad Zaman): can regime-aware machine learning improve a market-neutral pairs-trading strategy?
  • Built a clean daily-price dataset for 105 US large caps across 12 sectors (2010-2025) with a 95% coverage filter; ran Engle-Granger and Johansen cointegration tests on rolling windows and shortlisted 4 pairs (MA-V, ICE-V, JNJ-LLY, AXP-ICE).
  • Built daily features using same-day information only (with a variance-inflation check) and trained XGBoost, LightGBM and LSTM signal models, benchmarked against a static z-score cointegration rule.
  • Walk-forward out-of-sample backtest with transaction costs and a one-day execution delay, plus sensitivity analysis on costs (including 5 bps) and benchmark thresholds.
  • Findings: tree models classify well (macro-F1 0.76 and 0.75 vs 0.32 for the LSTM) but lose money when traded; mean out-of-sample Sharpe was -0.02 (benchmark), -0.31 (XGBoost), -0.38 (LightGBM), -0.41 (LSTM). High recall with low precision means over-trading, and costs erase the edge. Hypothesis rejected for this universe.
  • Takeaway: classification accuracy does not translate into trading profit. Limits are stated openly: only 4 pairs, a single validation split, current index constituents only.

Data Leakage & Walk-Forward Backtesting of Deep Learning Trading Models

Shows how data leakage inflates the apparent performance of MLP, LSTM and CNN-GAF models on SPY, then re-tests them with leak-free walk-forward backtesting.

Python PyTorch NumPy Backtesting
  • Built a 5-day-ahead direction classifier on 2,000 days of SPY returns (10 lagged returns as features, chronological 70/30 split).
  • Leakage demonstration: labels built from a global return quantile and features normalized on the full sample; coded a check counting training samples whose labels overlap the test period.
  • Implemented an MLP, an LSTM (NumPy, gates and BPTT) and a CNN on Gramian Angular Field images of return windows.
  • Strategy backtest with long/short signals, 5-day non-overlapping trades and 5 bps costs; metrics: total/annualized return, volatility, Sharpe, max drawdown, hit rate versus buy-and-hold. On the leaky setup the MLP and CNN lost money while buy-and-hold had a Sharpe of 1.31.
  • Walk-forward validation (500/500 and 500/100 windows) with scaling and labels computed inside each fold; retrained MLP and CNN-GAF in PyTorch per fold. Leak-free accuracy of about 59-60% mostly reflected majority-class prediction (class-1 recall of 1-3%).
  • Takeaway: headline accuracy hides weak predictive power; only leak-free walk-forward tests with trading metrics give an honest picture.

Exotic Option Pricing: Stochastic Volatility & Jump Diffusion

Pricing options under the Heston and Merton models via Monte Carlo simulation, extending to American early-exercise and barrier options; where theory meets the messiness of real option pricing.

Python Monte Carlo Heston Model Options Pricing
  • Heston model (stochastic volatility); simulated correlated stock price and variance processes via Monte Carlo (10,000 paths) to price at-the-money European calls and puts, testing sensitivity to the correlation parameter ρ between asset returns and volatility.
  • Merton jump-diffusion model; modeled sudden price jumps (Poisson-distributed) layered on top of standard diffusion, pricing options under varying jump intensities and comparing the resulting price curves against the Heston model's smoother, volatility-driven behavior.
  • Greeks estimation; computed Delta and Gamma numerically under both models to quantify option price sensitivity to the underlying asset.
  • American options & early exercise; priced an American call under the Heston model using Monte Carlo with Least Squares Regression (the Longstaff-Schwartz approach), confirming the early-exercise premium against the European price.
  • Barrier options; priced Up-and-In Call and Down-and-In Put exotic options under jump-diffusion, showing how barrier conditions can significantly discount an option relative to its vanilla European equivalent.

Python Hierarchical Risk Parity CPCV Risk Management
  • Implemented from scratch covariance denoising, Hierarchical Risk Parity (hierarchical clustering of assets) and Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation (many train/test combinations with purging and embargo to prevent leakage), on 10 US equities (Jan 2024 - Jun 2025).
  • Built 8 strategies combining these methods, with a 15%-capped Maximum Sharpe portfolio as baseline, and compared annual return, volatility, Sharpe, max drawdown, CVaR and weight stability.

Financial Econometrics: Diagnosing Data Pathologies

A practical framework for spotting and fixing the four statistical issues that quietly break financial models; skewness, fat tails, outlier sensitivity, and non-stationarity; with diagnosis and remedies for each.

Python Statsmodels Time Series
  • Skewness; simulated and measured distributional asymmetry, and discussed its impact on risk models that assume normality.
  • Kurtosis & heteroscedasticity; examined fat-tailed distributions and time-varying volatility, both common in financial returns and both dangerous when ignored in options pricing and risk management.
  • Outlier sensitivity; used the modified Z-score (median absolute deviation) to detect and quantify the distortion outliers introduce into means, variances, and regression coefficients.
  • Non-stationarity & unit root testing; applied the Augmented Dickey-Fuller test to detect random-walk behavior in time series, and discussed why modeling non-stationary data without differencing produces spurious, unreliable results.

Forecasting Oil Price Regimes: HMM Discretization & Bayesian Networks

Builds a leakage-aware pipeline to forecast next-month oil-price regimes, combining HMM state discretization of 13 market and macro variables with a Bayesian network learned by hill-climbing search.

Python Bayesian Network HMM pgmpy
  • Assembled monthly data from 2000 to 2026 on oil, equities, the USD, the VIX, energy ETFs and US macro series (Yahoo Finance, FRED), with a chronological 80/10/10 train/validation/test split.
  • Trained a 3-state Hidden Markov Model for each of the 13 variables to turn them into bear, stagnant and bull regimes, with the state labelling derived from the training set only.
  • Defined an expert-drafted causal graph and refined it with hill-climbing structure search (maximum 3 parents per node), comparing K2, BIC and BDeu scoring on a one-step-ahead forecast target.
  • Added a guardrail that flags implausibly good validation scores; the final near-perfect accuracy triggered it and is treated as a likely data-leakage artifact, not a real result. The trading simulation showed no gain over buy-and-hold, consistent with that reading.
  • Takeaway: a model that looks perfect usually has a leak, so the next step is to decode regimes causally and re-test.
